## 英译汉

1. Five major religions are practiced in the world today and each boasts a long history. **Hinduism** developed first,
   then **Buddhism**, **Judaism**, **Christianity**, and finally **Islam**. But they are not more or less important than
   other religions because they are older or newer. Their importance is that for centuries they have satisfied basic
   human needs and answered man's basic questions. The chief differences among them **have to do with** whose needs they
   satisfy and whose questions they answer. Hinduism and Buddhism are Eastern religions. Both arose in India, although
   Buddhism is practiced chiefly in Southeast Asia today. Hinduism is **polytheism** while Buddhism is a religion with
   no gods. In contrast, Judaism, Christianity, and Islam are Western religions. All the three religions are *
   *monotheistic** religions, stressing belief in one God. In terms of the number of **adherents**, Christianity is the
   biggest religion with more than two billion followers around the world.

   当今世界有五大宗教，每一个都有着悠久的历史。**印度教**最先发展起来，随后是**佛教**、**犹太教**、**基督教**，最后是**伊斯兰教
   **。但它们并不会因为诞生时间的早晚而比其他宗教更重要或更不重要。其重要性在于，几个世纪以来，它们满足了人类的基本需求，解答了人类的根本问题。它们之间的主要区别
   **在于**所满足的对象需求和所解答的对象问题不同。印度教和佛教属于东方宗教，二者都起源于印度，不过如今佛教主要在东南亚地区盛行。印度教是
   **多神教**，而佛教是无神宗教。相比之下，犹太教、基督教和伊斯兰教属于西方宗教，这三大宗教均为**一神教**，强调信仰唯一的神。从
   **信徒**数量来看，基督教是最大的宗教，全球信徒超过20亿。

2. **The Bible** is a **canonical** collection of religious writings in **Judaism** and **Christianity**, although the
   contents of each of the canonical texts are not the same. Christians call it the Holy Bible, since it is considered *
   *divine** as the word of God. The whole canonical text of the Bible is considered to be divinely inspired. Its
   authors may be led or influenced by God to write down "the word of God". The Christian Bible consists of two parts: *
   *the Old** **Testament** and the New Testament. The Old Testament is basically the same as the Bible of Judaism, or
   the Hebrew Bible. It tells of the **revelation** of God throughout history before the coming of **Jesus Christ**.
   Mostly written in **Hebrew**, it corresponds with the history of the Jewish people. The New Testament is the
   biography of Jesus. It **deals with** the stories and teachings of Jesus Christ. The Bible, the first mass-printed
   book in the West, has produced a major influence on Western civilization. It is widely considered to be the
   best-selling book of all time and the most translated work in the world.

   **《圣经》**是犹太教和基督教中宗教著作的**经典**合集，尽管各教派对经典文本的内容界定不尽相同。基督徒称之为“圣经”，因其被视为
   **神圣**的“上帝之言”。整部《圣经》正典文本被认为受神启示而成，其作者可能是在上帝的引导或影响下记录下“上帝的话语”。基督教《圣经》由两部分组成：
   **《旧约》**和《新约》。《旧约》与犹太教的《圣经》（即**《希伯来圣经》**）基本一致，讲述了**耶稣基督**降临之前上帝在历史中的**启示
   **，大部分以**希伯来文**写成，与犹太民族的历史相对应。《新约》是耶稣的传记，内容**涉及**
   耶稣基督的事迹和教导。作为西方首部大规模印刷的书籍，《圣经》对西方文明产生了深远影响，被广泛认为是有史以来最畅销的书籍，也是世界上翻译版本最多的作品。

3. **Socrates** was a great ancient Greek philosopher, thinker, and educator, who is considered as one of the *
   *forerunners** of Western philosophy. He is an **enigmatic** figure known chiefly through the accounts of later
   classical writers, especially the writings of his most famous student Plato. Socrates is renowned for his
   contribution to the field of ethics. He also made important and lasting contributions to the field of **epistemology
   ** and logic, and the influence of his ideas and approaches remains a strong foundation for Western philosophy that
   followed. He is best recognized for inventing the teaching practice of **pedagogy**, where in a teacher questions a
   student in a manner that draws out the correct response. His method of teaching by asking and answering questions to
   stimulate critical thinking and to **illuminate** ideas, known as the Socratic Method, remains a commonly used tool
   in a wide range of discussions. Socrates was the most **colorful** figure in the history of ancient philosophy. His
   fame was widespread **in his own time**, and his name soon became a household word although he professed no
   extraordinary wisdom, constructed no philosophical system, and established no **school**.

   **苏格拉底**是古希腊伟大的哲学家、思想家和教育家，被视作西方哲学的**先驱**之一。他是一位**颇具神秘色彩的**
   人物，其形象主要通过后世古典作家的记述为世人所知，尤其是他最著名的学生柏拉图的著作。苏格拉底以其在伦理学领域的贡献闻名于世，同时也在
   **认识论**和逻辑学领域做出了重要且深远的贡献，其思想与方法的影响至今仍是西方哲学的重要根基。他最为人熟知的成就是开创了一种
   **教学方法**，即教师通过提问的方式引导学生得出正确答案。他这种通过问答来激发批判性思维、**阐释**
   观点的教学方法被称为“苏格拉底方法”，至今仍在广泛的讨论中被普遍使用。苏格拉底是古代哲学史上最**富传奇色彩的**人物，他在*
   *当时**便已声名远扬，尽管他自称没有超凡的智慧、未构建哲学体系、也未创立**学派**，但他的名字很快就家喻户晓。

4. **Jean-Paul Sartre** was a key representative figure in the philosophy of **existentialism**, and one of the leading
   figures in 20th-century French philosophy. Apart from being a philosopher, he is also known as a **playwright**,
   novelist, political activist, biographer, and literary critic. In 1938 Sartre wrote the novel ***Nausea*** which
   serves in some ways as a **manifesto** of existentialism and remains one of his most famous books. His philosophical
   work ***Being and Nothingness*** in 1943 is a representative work on existentialism, which occupies an important
   position in the history of philosophy. Sartre's primary philosophical idea is that people, as humans, are "*
   *condemned** to be free". Hence, people are necessarily fully responsible for their actions and behaviors. In
   addition, Sartre is perhaps best known as a playwright. Some of his plays include ***The Flies*** and ***No Exit***,
   which are richly **symbolic** and serve as a means of conveying his philosophy. In 1964 he was awarded the Nobel
   Prize in Literature but refused it, saying that he always declined official honors. He was the first Nobel **Laureate
   ** to voluntarily decline the prize

   **让 - 保罗·萨特**是存在主义哲学的关键代表人物，也是20世纪法国哲学的领军人物之一。除了作为哲学家，他还以**剧作家**
   、小说家、政治活动家、传记作家和文学批评家的身份为人熟知。1938年，萨特创作了小说**《恶心》**，这部作品在某种程度上可视为存在主义的
   **宣言**，至今仍是他最著名的作品之一。1943年，他的哲学著作**《存在与虚无》**是存在主义的代表作，在哲学史上占据重要地位。萨特的核心哲学观点是，人作为人类“被
   **判定**为自由”，因此必须对自己的行为和举止负全部责任。此外，萨特或许作为剧作家最为人熟知，他的一些戏剧作品包括**《苍蝇》*
   *和**《禁闭》**，这些作品**充满象征意义**，成为传达其哲学思想的重要载体。1964年，他被授予诺贝尔文学奖，但他拒绝了这一奖项，称自己始终拒绝官方荣誉。他是首位自愿拒绝诺贝尔奖的
   **获奖者**。

## 必讲篇目

1. The 25th day of December is one of the most important traditional Western festivals — Christmas. It **commemorates**
   （纪念） the birth of Jesus Christ. The festival dates from as early as the 4th century. Not until the Middle Ages did
   the feast of Christmas become widespread. Over time it **incorporates** many pre-Christian, **pagan**（异教徒）
   traditions into the festivities. Gradually it evolved into a religious as well as **secular** （世俗的）celebration.
   Christians celebrate it for its religious meaning, while non-Christians are fond of it for its festive atmosphere.
   Today Christmas is observed as an important festival and public holiday in many countries around the world. Christmas
   customs vary from country to country. Popular modern customs of the holiday include an exchange of Christmas cards
   and gifts, Christmas music and **caroling**（圣诞颂歌）, church attendance, the display of various Christmas
   decorations and trees, family gatherings, and a special meal .Decorating a Christmas tree is one of the most
   well-known traditions of the festivities. To small children, the festival is full of fantasy and surprise. Legend has
   it that Santa Claus will enter each house through the **chimney**（烟囱） and bring gifts to well-behaved children on
   Christmas Eve. The tradition of hanging stockings is for receiving the gifts from Santa Claus.
2. Easters the most important Christian festival celebrating the **resurrection**（复活） of Jesus Christ. Its religious
   significance surpasses that of Christmas. Christians **commemorate** the festival as the day when Jesus Christ was *
   *resurrected** three days after he had been **crucified**（钉在十字架）. For Christians, Easter signifies a time of *
   *miracles**（奇迹、神迹） and a **reaffirming**（重申） of faith, marking rebirth and hope. Unlike other holidays, it is a
   **movable** holiday and not fixed on the calendar. It falls on a Sunday between March 22 and April 25, the first
   Sunday after the full moon that occurs after the **spring** **equinox**（春分）. Christians typically go to church on
   Easter Sunday, but there are other traditions as well. For many people the Easter egg is the main symbol of Easter.
   During the festival eggs are beautifully colored and given as gifts. Chocolate eggs, either hollow or having a
   filling, are children's beloved gifts. The Easter bunny is a popular legendary character, who brings baskets filled
   with colored eggs, chocolates, and toys to the homes of children on Easter morning. Many families have Easter egg
   hunts, in which decorated or chocolate eggs are hidden around the home or garden for children to find . Children who
   find the most win the prize.
3. The Double Ninth Festival (also called the Chongyang Festival), observed on the ninth day of the ninth lunar month,
   is a traditional Chinese holiday. The festival dates back to the Warring States Period, and by the Tang Dynasty it
   became an official festival, passed on to the present time. As “double ninth" is pronounced the same as the word "
   jiujiu" in Chinese, which signifies "forever" and "longevity", the day is considered an **auspicious**（吉祥的） day
   worth celebrating. There are various activities to celebrate the festival, including **ascending**（登高）mountains,
   admiring the beauty of **chrysanthemums**（菊花）, wearing the zhuyu plant, eating Chongyang cake, and drinking
   chrysanthemum wine. Ancient literary figures left many famous poems celebrating the Chongyang Festival and **odes**
   （颂诗） to the chrysanthemum. Despite all the different customs of celebration, safety and harmony has remained the
   festival's core cultural value for over 2,000 years. In 1988, the Chinese government decided the ninth day of the
   ninth lunar month as **Senior Citizens' Day**（敬老节）, advocating social respect, care, and help for the elderly
   people. In 2013, the Chinese government made Senior Citizens' Day a legal holiday, showing the government's loving
   concern for the elderly people. Today various activities will be organized for elderly people on the day, for
   example, offering free medical treatment and holding exhibitions of calligraphy, paintings, and photos for them.
4. The moon cake, a traditional food popular with the Chinese people, is regarded as an indispensable **delicacy** of
   the Mid-Autumn Festival. The moon cake is a kind of round cake with various fillings and different artistic patterns
   on the surface depicting the legends of the festival. In Chinese culture, the round shape symbolizes completeness and
   unity. Thus, the sharing of round moon cakes among family members signifies the unity of the family. The custom of
   eating moon cakes is said to originate from the Tang Dynasty. In the Northern Song Dynasty, the cake was called "the
   palace cake", popular in the palace and **finding its way among** the ordinary people. By the Ming Dynasty, eating
   moon cakes during the Mid-Autumn Festival became a nationwide custom. As time goes by, the moon cakes have been
   greatly diversified in category and flavor - Beijing- style, Suzhou-style, Canton-style, Chaozhou- style, **to name
   only a few**. Nowadays, although making moon cakes at home has given way to the more popular custom of giving moon
   cakes to relatives and close friends, the original intention of maintaining family unity remains.
5. The Statue of Liberty, located on Liberty Island in New York, is one of the most famous tourist sites in New York
   City. The Statue, dedicated on October 28, 1886, was a gift to the United States from the people of France in honor
   of the **centennial**（百周年纪念） of American independence in recognition of the friendship established during the
   American Revolution. The Statue was designed by French **sculptor** Frederic Auguste Bartholdi and completed in
   collaboration with French engineer Alexandre Gustave Eiffel (designer of the Eiffel Tower). Meanwhile, the Statue was
   a joint effort between America and France. It was agreed upon that the American people were to build the **pedestal**
   （底座）, and the French people were responsible for the Statue and its assembly in the United States. Its design and
   construction was recognized as one of the greatest technical achievements of the 19th century, and the Statue was
   seen as a masterpiece of human creativity. Over the years, it has become a symbol of the United States and its
   symbolism has grown to include freedom and **democracy** as well as international friendship. In 1984, the Statue of
   Liberty was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. From 1984 to 1986, the Statue underwent a major restoration. On
   July 5, 1986, the newly restored Statue re-opened to the public, which celebrated her centennial.
6. **The Louvre**（卢浮宫）, located on the right bank of the Seine and in the center of Paris, is one of the world's
   biggest museums, famous for its collection of classical paintings and sculptures. Construction of the Louvre began in
   late 12th century, originally built as a defensive **fortress**（堡垒、要塞）, and later as a royal palace until Louis
   XIV chose **Versailles**（凡尔赛） as his residence in 1682. The building was extended many times to form the present
   Louvre Palace. In 1793, the Louvre was converted into a national art museum and opened to the public the same year.
   Today, the museum contains some 35,000 artworks on display, housing some of the most famous works of art including
   the Mona Lisa, the Venus de Milo, and the Winged Victory1 of Samothrace. The Louvre has become the world's famous
   palace of art. With about nine million visitors each year , the Louvre is the world's most visited museum In 1981,
   French President Francois Mitterrand announced a plan to restore the Louvre Palace. The pyramid entrance to the
   museum by the Chinese-American architect Ieoh Ming Pei adds to the **glamor**（魅力） of the Louvre and becomes a new
   shining point of the museum.
7. The Great Wall, one of the greatest wonders of the world, was listed as a UNESCO World Cultural Heritage Site in
    1987. Like a giant dragon, the Great Wall **winds its way** across grasslands, deserts and mountains, stretching
          approximately 8,851.8 kilometers (the Ming walls) from west to east of China. With a history of more than
          2,000
          years, some of the sections are now in ruins or have disappeared. However, it is still one of the most
          appealing
          attractions all around the world owing to its architectural **grandeur**（宏伟） and historical significance.
          The Great
          Wall was originally built in the Spring and Autumn Period and the Warring States Period as a defensive project
          by
          states including Yan, Zhao, and Qin. Emperor Qinshihuang, founder of the first unified empire in Chinese
          history,
          succeeded in his effort to have the walls joined together to **fend** （防御）off the invasions from tribes in
          the
          north. Since then, the Great Wall has served as a **monument** of the Chinese nation. It went through constant
          extensions and repairs in later dynasties. The Great Wall we see today was mostly built during the Ming
          Dynasty. The
          Great Wall carries a considerable part of Chinese culture and demonstrates the wisdom and **tenacity**（坚韧）
          of the
          Chinese people. It has become a symbol of the Chinese nation along with the Yellow River and the Yangtze
          River.
8. The Ancient City of Pingyao is located in Pingyao County in central Shanxi Province. It is a famous historic and
   cultural city with a history of over 2,700 years, and also one of the four great ancient cities in China. The Ancient
   City of Pingyao is an exceptionally well-preserved ancient county-level city in China and an outstanding example of
   traditional Han Chinese cities in the Ming and Qing dynasties. It presents an extraordinary and complete picture of
   the cultural, social, economic, and religious development from the 14th to 20th century. A history of 2,700 years has
   left the city with numerous historic buildings and human landscapes. Pingyao is known as "a treasure house of Chinese
   classic architecture" and **enjoys the reputation of** “museum of architecture of the Ming and Qing dynasties". The
   ancient city walls, Zhenguo Temple, and Shuanglin Temple are referred to as “Three Treasures of Pingyao". In the 19th
   and early 20th centuries Pingyao became the financial center of the country. The existing shops and traditional *
   *dwellings**（住宅） **bear witness to**（为...作证） its prosperity in the past. In 1997 the Ancient City of Pingyao was
   **inscribed**（写在） on the World Heritage List. As a famous historic and cultural city, Pingyao attracts numerous
   tourists every year.
9. Oil painting is an important genre of Western painting. It is to paint on the canvas or other materials with *
   *pigments**（颜料） that are bound with a medium of drying oil. The widely-known advantages of oil painting include a
   three-dimensional effect and the **sheen**（光泽） that lasts for a long time. The most world-famous work of oil
   painting is undoubtedly the Mona Lisa by the Italian artist Leonardo da Vinci. The painting is a portrait of a woman
   who was seated elegantly, her hands quietly folded, gazing directly at the viewer, with a slight and subtle smile on
   her face. The detail of the painting, especially the **enigmatic** expression on the woman's face, is where the
   painting's charm lies, which has aroused diverse interpretations over the past 500 years and has contributed to the
   continuing fascination of the work . It has been acclaimed as the best-known, the most visited, the most written
   about, and the most reproduced work of art in the world. The painting is on permanent display at the Louvre Museum in
   Paris.
10. Jazz is a unique contribution of America to world arts and culture. Initially a musical form belonging to the lower
    class of America, jazz was gradually modified and accepted by all people in America and widespread around the globe.
    Jazz was created in New Orleans, a port city in the south of the US at the turn of the 20th century. The creation of
    jazz was much affected by blues, which has always been an indispensable part of jazz. Ragtime, popular in the 1890s
    in the south, also influenced the creation of jazz. The 1920s is regarded as the Jazz Age, or the Golden Age of
    American Jazz. The later years witnessed the development of different and complicated types of jazz and the
    emergence of a number of world- renowned jazz masters. Unlike other genres of music, jazz has a unique feature — *
    *improvisation**（即兴）. Performers of jazz write down their own creative notes to music as they play. Thus, even
    when different jazz musicians play the same piece, it can sound fresh and different. For this reason, jazz shows the
    free spirit of individualism via musical improvisation.
11. The traditional Chinese knot, one of the most popular traditional folk arts in China, was first invented by
    craftsmen and has now become an elegant art after hundreds of years of improvement. In ancient times, people used it
    to record events, but nowadays it is used mainly for decoration. Red is a prevalent color for the traditional
    Chinese knot. "Knot" in Chinese stands for love, marriage, and reunion. The traditional Chinese knot is usually used
    as a gift or **ornament**（装饰品）, which is believed to bring good luck and eliminate ill fortune. Handed down from
    generation to generation, the art of traditional Chinese knot is becoming increasingly popular in China as well as
    in the rest of the world.
12. **Cloisonne** is a traditional craft widely known in and outside China. It is a kind of superb local expertise of
    Beijing, which combines the techniques of **bronze**（青铜） and **porcelain**（瓷器） and the skills of traditional
    Chinese painting and carving. The making of cloisonne gained great popularity during the Jingtai reign (1450-1456)
    of the Ming Dynasty, with the main color used being blue, hence the name “Jingtai Blue", or cloisonne as it became
    known later on. Cloisonne was further developed during the Qing Dynasty. **Technically**, it overcame some of the
    weaknesses of the Ming cloisonne, but it lacked the latter's simplicity and **dignified**（端庄的） heaviness. *
    *Glittering**（闪闪发光的） and elegant, exquisite and extravagant, it is **hailed**（赞扬） as a national treasure with
    a distinctive national style and profound cultural **connotations**. In 2006, it was listed into the first batch of
    National Intangible Cultural Heritage List. Nowadays, cloisonne is **deemed** valuable in the eyes of collectors; it
    also provides **refined**（精致的） ornaments for daily use.
13. **The Colosseum**（罗马斗兽场） is a vast **elliptical**（椭圆的） **amphitheater**（竞技场） of ancient Rome. Made of
    concrete and stone, it is located east of the Roman Forum, in the center of Italy. As the largest amphitheater in
    the world, it is a vast **ellipse**（椭圆） of over 500 meters in **perimeter**（周长）, the Colosseum has four stories
    and each of the first three stories has 80 **arched**（拱形的） entrances, which enabled spectators to enter and exit
    in good order. The central **oval**（椭圆） **arena**（竞技场） is able to accommodate some 50,000 spectators who may
    have been arranged according to social ranking. The arena was designed mainly for **gladiatorial**（角斗） contests
    and other public activities. The Colosseum is deemed to be one of the greatest works of Roman architecture and
    engineering and an **iconic** symbol of Imperial Rome. Modern sports stadiums are influenced by the design of the
    Colosseum in one way or another. It was listed as a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 1980. Today it is one of the
    most popular tourist attractions in Italy, playing host to visitors from all around the world
14. Siheyuan (courtyard house) and hutong (alley), the two construction characteristics of Beijing, have long become the
    symbol of the city. As a traditional Chinese building, siheyuan is an **enclosed**（封闭的） courtyard surrounded by
    high walls, with an enclosed space and an entrance opening to the outside. The most amazing part of the courtyard is
    its integration with nature as its structure and layout **embody**（表现） traditional Chinese philosophy — the
    harmony between man and nature. Neighboring blocks of siheyuan of different scales form **passages**（通道） **in
    between**（介于之间）, which are called hutong. The hutong culture has become an integral part of the ancient capital
    culture of Beijing. Today, with the change in family structure and social values, siheyuan and hutong are both
    facing the challenge of survival. In the process of urban planning, both are caught in the dilemma of protection and
    development. Some siheyuan and hutongs are designated as cultural sites under protection while some are **demolished
    **（拆毁）. The **extant**（现存的） hutongs have been developed into rare resources of tourism.
15. Greek **mythology**（神话） is the body of oral or written myths that belong to the ancient Greeks, concerning their
    gods, heroes, and the nature of the world. It was a part of the religion in ancient Greece and the earliest form of
    literature of Europe. Greek myths attempt to explain the origins of the world, and the lives and adventures of a
    wide variety of gods, goddesses, heroes, heroines, and mythological creatures. These accounts were initially *
    *disseminated**（散播） in an oral tradition. Today the Greek myths are known primarily from ancient Greek literature.
    The oldest known Greek literary sources concerning Greek myths were Iliad and Odyssey, the epic poems traditionally
    **attributed** to Homer. Mythology was **at the heart of** everyday life in Ancient Greece. Greeks regarded
    mythology as a part of their history. Greek mythology has had an extensive influence on Western culture, arts, and
    literature. It remains an important part of Western cultural heritage. Poets and artists from ancient times to the
    present have derived inspiration from Greek mythology and have discovered contemporary significance in the themes.
    The study of Greek myths can help **throw light on** the religious and political institutions of Ancient Greece and
    its civilization, and gain a better understanding of the nature of myth making itself
16. Jane Austen, one of England's foremost novelists, was never publicly acknowledged as a writer during her lifetime .
    Jane Austen wrote six novels, which continue to **captivate**（使着迷） readers almost 200 years after her death:
    Sense and Sensibility, Pride and Prejudice, Northanger Abbey, Mansfield Park, Emma, and Persuasion. Her realism,
    biting **irony**, and social **commentary**（评论） have gained her historical importance among scholars and critics .
    Her plots, though fundamentally comic, highlight the dependence of women on marriage to secure social standing and
    economic security. Her works, though usually popular, were first published anonymously and brought her little
    personal fame and only a few positive reviews during her lifetime, but the publication in 1869 of her nephew's A
    Memoir of Jane Austen introduced her to a wider public, and by the 1940s she had become widely accepted in *
    *academia**（学术界） as a great English writer . The second half of the 20th century saw a **proliferation**（激增） of
    Austen scholarship and the emergence of a Janeite fan culture.
17. **The Book of Songs** is the first **anthology**（选集） of Chinese poetry. It contains 305 poems created over a
    period of some 500 years from the early Western Zhou Dynasty to the middle of the Spring and Autumn Period. In
    contents it is composed of three parts, namely feng, ya, and song. Feng is mostly local folk songs, ya the royal
    court songs, and **ancestral temple**（宗祠） songs. The Book of Songs has provided a deep insight into all aspects of
    social life of the Zhou Dynasty and truly reflected the rise and fall of the Chinese slave society. As one of the
    main sources of Chinese literature, The Book of Songs has always been **worshipped**（爱慕、崇拜） by scholars and has
    become a cultural gene. It signals the **onset**（开端） of the Chinese narrative and lyric poems as well as the

27. **Acupuncture**（针灸）, one of the oldest medical procedures in the world, is a traditional Chinese medical technique
    involving the use of needles to manipulate a person's chi for the purpose of restoring the balance between **the
    opposing forces** of chi and yang in the body. Legend has it that acupuncture originated from the period of Huang
    Di, known as the Yellow Emperor. According to **folklore**（民间传说）, Huang Di brought the knowledge of things like
    bows and arrows, writing, the calendar, and acupuncture. He also contributed to a medical text called Yellow
    Emperor's Classic of Internal Medicine which is still in use today. This book introduces the idea of chi and its
    importance to a person's mental and physical health. Evidence of the use of acupuncture goes back at least 4,000
    years in China, although the oldest surviving texts date only from 2,000 years ago. According to the principle which
    lies behind the use of acupuncture, illness will occur when a person's chi fails to move properly along the internal
    **meridians**（经络）. Needles are inserted at specific points along these meridians in order to release **blockages**
    （堵塞） and restore the balance between chi and yang.
28. **The imperial civil examination system**（科举制度） was an examination system that the Chinese feudal dynasties used

    system, educational system, and humanistic ideologies. There is no doubt that, compared with the **hereditary**（世袭的）
    system, the imperial civil examination was a relatively fair and open method to select officials. It improved the
    system of selecting officials and promoted the development of education. However, the imperial civil examination
    system also had negative effects. Especially in the Ming and Qing dynasties, the contents of the test were limited
    to the Four Books and the Five Classics. All candidates had to write a composition explaining their views on an idea
    from these books in a rigid form and structure consisting of eight parts, which was called baguwen (Eight-part
    Essay). To some degree, its rigid content and form of examination bound people's thinking and limited cultural
    progress.
